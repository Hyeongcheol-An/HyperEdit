## Release notes

⌘↓ reaches the end of the document again after a fold that covers the end has opened.

### Fixed

- **⌘↓ into a fold that reaches the end of the document** — with ⌘↓ from the header of such a fold, the fold opened and the caret went to the end, but in documents of short lines the view could stay at the top; the same happened with ⌘↓ right after unfolding such a region. The view now goes to the end.

### For contributors

- After a fold that reaches the end of the document has opened, the fragment of the last paragraph is still where it was while folded, right below the header: the paragraphs in between were never enumerated, and TextKit 2 keeps it there until it revises its height estimate. AppKit then takes the end of the document for visible and does not scroll. `HyperTextView.revealDocumentEndIfMissed` checks against the laid-out viewport, not against coordinates, and pins the view to the end. It only shows when the estimate already matches the text, so it depends on what was scrolled before: the full harness run failed every time, the scenario alone never did (`--only scroll-knob-state,fold-giant` shows it on the old code).

### Compatibility

- macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later. The DMG (`HyperEdit-<version>-universal.dmg`) contains one universal binary that runs natively on Apple Silicon (arm64) and Intel (x86_64) Macs.

## [0.11.4](https://github.com/Hyeongcheol-An/HyperEdit/compare/v0.11.3...v0.11.4) (2026-11-26)

* **editor:** scroll by one line when typing at the edge; trackpad, knob and document switch ([#30](https://github.com/Hyeongcheol-An/HyperEdit/issues/30)) ([f7d0eb3](https://github.com/Hyeongcheol-An/HyperEdit/commit/f7d0eb3ab84e90cde076240e9752a5e746c7ac8f))


## Release notes

Typing past the edge of the window scrolls by one line instead of half a screen, trackpad scrolling no longer fights the bounce at the ends of a document, the scroll bar knob shows where you are and no longer moves backwards, and switching to a shorter document no longer freezes the app.

### Fixed

**Typing and moving the caret past the edge**

- **Return and typing on the last visible line** — pressing Return on the last visible line, or typing until that line wrapped, moved the view by half a screen (213 pt and 192 pt in a 382 pt editor). The view now moves by just the line that left the screen (24 pt). The same goes for Hangul composition that wraps the line and for deleting at the head of the first visible line (it jumped 130–183 pt). Right after a very long line too: Return at the end of a 26,000-character line that ends on the bottom line moved the content by 202 pt and now moves it by one line.
- **Return one line above the bottom** — it jumped by 189 pt although the new line fitted on the screen. The view no longer moves.
- **With word wrap off** — there the arrow keys jumped as well: ↓ or Return on the bottom line and ↑ or Backspace on the top line moved the view by half a screen (192 pt of 382). They now move it by one line. Sideways, the view follows a caret that crosses the left edge by as much as it crossed, and brings a caret that crosses the right edge to the middle of the visible width, as before. A selection that an edit leaves behind — a snippet placeholder, a clause of an input method — is brought into view whole when it fits, and a horizontal scroll bar that appears while the view follows the caret no longer covers the caret's line.
- **After a large paste** — the caret could end up just outside the window: 182 pt below it after pasting 600 lines into the middle of a 1.3 MB note. The editor now checks once more, after the layout has settled, that the caret is on screen.
- **Replaying a macro** — the view followed the caret after every step of a replay; it now follows once per screen update and once at the end. A hundred runs of a 10-step macro on a 1.2 MB document take about 0.65 s (0.72–0.74 s before).
- **Gutter width when the line count gains or loses a digit** — the line-number gutter took its new width one layout pass after the edit. In a window completely covered by other windows that pass did not come, and the gutter's frame stayed at the old width (69 pt for a 62 pt gutter after going from 10,000 to 9,999 lines) until something else laid the window out. The width is now set together with the edit.
- Typing costs the same as before.

**Trackpad**

- **Bounce at the ends** — swiping past the end of a document with a trackpad made the text jump back by 16–82 pt on every frame of the bounce and left the view 5–71 pt short of the end, with the last line cut off. At the top it came to rest 5–19 pt below the first line. The view now stretches once and comes back to exactly the end.
- **Typing while the view is coasting** — typing or pressing an arrow key during the momentum of a swipe brought the caret into view, and the rest of the momentum then carried the view away again; the typed text ended up 1,375 pt off screen. When the gesture ends, the caret is brought back into view.

**Scroll bar knob**

- **Knob moving the wrong way** — while wheeling down through a 1.3 MB note the knob went backwards on 95 of 250 steps (by up to 14.6 pt on a 382 pt track), and after Go to Line it sat 2–4 % away from where that line is in the document. The knob is now placed by line: the same screen always gives the same position and it moves only the way you scroll. In the same run the largest backward move is 0.9 pt, and the lines at a quarter, half and three quarters of the document put the knob at 0.2499, 0.5000 and 0.7502.
- **Dragging the knob** — dragging the knob past the top of the track did not reach the first line (it stopped about 40,000 pt short in the 1.3 MB note); it does now. The same drag distance moves the same number of lines wherever you are, and dragging down and back up by the same amount returns to where you started.
- The markers in the strip at the right edge use the same scale as the knob, so the markers of the lines on screen lie within the knob.

**Switching documents**

- **Switching from the end of a long document to a shorter one** — opening a file, or switching to a tab, whose document is shorter than the place you were looking at in the previous one made the editor lay out the whole new document: 17.5 s for a 12,002-line JSON file opened from the end of a 24,000-line file. Large edits in that document were slow afterwards as well (Format JSON 3.1 s instead of 150 ms). The switch now takes 160 ms and Format JSON 166 ms. With a split editor it makes no difference which of the two editors was at the end.
- **A file rewritten shorter by another program** — while you looked at the end of a 24,002-line JSON file and it was rewritten with 12,002 lines, reading it again took 16.2 s and the next Format JSON 3.2 s. They now take 272 ms and 147 ms. A file that grows — a log being appended to — stays where you were reading, as before, and so does a reload while you read the middle of the file.
- **The other editor of a split during a whole-document edit** — when one editor of a split replaced the document (Select All and then a tool or a paste, or the undo or redo of that), the other editor kept its scroll offset while every height estimate changed: in a 24,000-line document it slid from line 12,002 to line 11,541, and from the end to 2,275 lines above it. It now stays on its line, and an editor that was showing the end stays at the end.

### Known limitations

- An edit that sends the caret more than a screen away (a large paste), typing on the last line of the document, undo, find jumps and Go to Line still bring the caret's line to the middle of the window, as before.
- With several carets, typing while one of them is above the visible part of the document can still shift the text slightly (4–33 pt at once): TextKit re-estimates the heights above the screen after such an edit.
- The caret comes back when the momentum has ended, not at once: macOS offers no public way to stop its momentum scrolling.
- A paragraph that wraps over several lines counts as one line, so the knob moves more slowly through a stretch of long paragraphs.
- With word wrap off, or while anything is folded, the knob is placed as before.
- A wheel step larger than the window (800 pt in a 382 pt editor) that falls on the step where TextKit discards its layout cache can still move the text 22–61 lines backwards; the knob follows the text.

### For contributors

- `insertText`, `setMarkedText` and the `insert…` / `delete…` commands mark the reveal they cause (`editRevealDepth`), and `scrollRangeToVisible` then uses the minimal reveal of the arrow keys (`revealCaretStep(afterEdit:)`) — in both wrap modes; with wrap off the sideways position is settled right after it. Right after an edit the edited paragraph is back to an estimated height, so the caret line measured 24 pt lower than it is, and laying fragments out with `.ensuresLayout` does not correct the positions below. `caretLineAfterEdit` adds up the real heights from the edited paragraph to the caret's.
- That sum is used only when the edit starts inside the laid-out viewport. From a second caret above the screen, in text that was never laid out, the caret line came out 295–1,900 pt off and typing one character threw the view up to 2,052 pt away; such edits are left to AppKit.
- It covers up to 64 paragraphs. Beyond that — and, for an edit that replaces the document, when a paragraph over the huge-paragraph limit lies on the way — the edit is left to AppKit and the caret is checked again on the next turn (`revealCaretAfterRelayout`). An edit that starts on screen measures such a paragraph as well: it is laid out for drawing anyway, and skipping it handed Return right after a 26,000-character line back to AppKit's centring (202 pt instead of 24). Measuring far edits with a viewport layout walked the whole document after a whole-document edit (Toggle Comment on 1.3 MB went from 150 ms to 1.4 s) — a regression that the typing and scrolling scenarios did not show and the self-test's `perf` lines did. Whole-document edits keep the first ordinary paragraphs of the edit laid out, which is what stops the next layout pass from walking the document.
- After a reveal the editor handled itself, the sideways position is settled for the caret, for the end of the selection an arrow key moved, or — after an edit — for the whole selection from its end back to its start. The visible height is read again on every pass of that reveal: with always-visible scrollers a horizontal scroller can appear in the middle of it and make the view 17 pt shorter.
- The gutter learned that the line count had gained or lost a digit while drawing and only then asked its container for a layout, so its frame followed one pass late — and a window that is fully covered has no display cycle of its own to run that pass. `updateThickness()` is now called from `textDidChange`, and for the other editor of a split when the edit is passed on to it. `typing-10000-lines` checks the width in the turn of the edit, before anything is drawn, and the frame after one layout pass, so it does not depend on whether the window is visible.
- During a macro replay `scrollRangeToVisible` does nothing; `MacroRecorder` calls `revealCaretAfterReplaySteps` before each yield to the run loop and at the end. Inside one run-loop turn the paragraphs edited earlier are back at estimated heights, so following on every step scrolled to a wrong place, laid the viewport out and scrolled back (844–880 ms for the hundred runs before this change).
- Real-use scenarios in `RealUse+ScrollEdge.swift`: `typing-edge-bottom`, `-top`, `-end`, `-cost`, `-huge-line`, `-nowrap`, `-multicaret`, `typing-big-tools`, `-long-head`, `-redo`, `typing-macro-cost` and `typing-paste-empty`; `nowrap-snippet` and `nowrap-legacy-scroller` in `RealUse+NoWrap.swift`. `typing-macro-cost` compares the replay with the same 1,000 steps sent one by one in the same run, because absolute times move with the load of the machine. `typing-big-tools-redo` records that undo and redo of a whole-document tool are slow in the edit itself (5.3 s for Toggle Comment on 1.3 MB): the screen settles within 3 ms, so it is not a scrolling cost.
- The editor pulls the scroll position back into the document after layout, except during a trackpad gesture. It asked on every live-scroll notification whether `NSApp.currentEvent` was a phased scroll event; that is true for the first one only — the later ones come from the block that synchronizes responsive scrolling with the main thread, where there is no current event. The gesture is now recognized once, in `willStartLiveScroll`, and remembered until it ends (`liveScrollIsGesture`).
- `HyperTextView.liveScrollDidEnd` repeats a reveal of the caret that was requested during the gesture, if the caret line is not visible by then. The request lives only inside that gesture: it is dropped when the next live scroll starts and when the document is replaced — a knob drag sends the live-scroll notifications too, and a request left over from a gesture that never reported its end snapped the view back to the caret. Only reveals of the caret count; restoring a tab's reading place goes through `scrollToReadingPlace`, which says so, because its range can coincide with the caret (a tab left at the start of its document).
- The harness can now produce trackpad gestures. Handed to `scrollWheel(with:)` one by one, everything after the first phased event is ignored and the scroll view stays in a live scroll; posting the whole finger phase (began → changed… → ended) to the app's own event queue with `NSApp.postEvent` works — AppKit's tracker takes the queued events and then runs its own momentum and rubber-band in real time. The momentum check lets a frame carry what the frame before it fell short of, but only when that frame took more than one and a half periods and the clip view carried the catch-up as well: a late frame tends to follow a heavy layout pass, which is also where coordinates get re-estimated, so an unconditional allowance could cover a real jump.
- Synthetic wheel events must not carry the modifier keys the user happens to hold. The harness builds them with a `CGEvent` that has no source, and such an event takes the modifier keys that are down at that moment; AppKit turns a line wheel event that carries Shift into a horizontal scroll (deltaX −1, deltaY 0), so the notch moves nothing vertically. The flags are now cleared when the event is made. This fits the notches that moved nothing in `scroll-line-wheel` — one or a few in a row, only for line events, with the previous release's editor code as well, and in none of 44 runs while nobody was typing — but it could not be shown directly, since that needs someone to hold Shift during a run. For a notch that still does not move the view the scenario records the live-scroll notifications that arrived, whether the movement came within two seconds, whether the window was occluded and which modifier keys were down.
- Real-use scenarios `scroll-trackpad-swipe`, `-ends`, `-interrupt`, `-long` and `-stale-reveal` (`RealUse+Trackpad.swift`): 16 issues before, none after.
- `EditorScrollView` and `EditorScroller` (`Editor/EditorScrollView.swift`). `NSScrollView` places the knob at scroll position ÷ (document height − visible height), but the TextKit 2 document height is an estimate that is revised on every viewport layout — early in a session it swung between 847,845 and 1,157,051 pt for a note about 700,000 pt tall. The knob value is now the paragraphs above the screen divided by those above plus those below, read from the laid-out viewport and the live line index; a drag is translated back the same way (`scrollToLineValue`). Two traps: a scroller's `enclosingScrollView` is nil, and an `NSScroller` subclass must override `isCompatibleWithOverlayScrollers` to keep the overlay style.
- A knob drag sends the live-scroll start and end notifications like any other scroll. The line-based drag is taken with overlay and with always-visible scrollers, and typing does not make an overlay scroller appear although the knob value changes with the line count.
- Real-use scenarios `scroll-knob-drag`, `scroll-knob-position` and `scroll-knob-state` (`RealUse+ScrollKnob.swift`), with a time budget per drag step and a limit on content going backwards; `scroll-knob` went from 2 issues to none.
- The text is replaced in place when a document comes in, but the scroll position is still the previous one. Past the end of a shorter document, the first layout pass lays out the viewport there and TextKit 2 walks every paragraph from the top (see the notes on reflowing at the end of a document); the rendering attributes it leaves on every paragraph then make the spell checker walk them on each large edit. This is what failed the self-test check `format JSON on 1.3 MB is not a hang`.
- Another document: `HyperTextView.leaveScrollPositionForNewDocument` puts the view at the start just before the text changes — where the document changes its address, the place that also forgets the measured no-wrap width. It must not hang on the storage's "whole text edited" notification: Fold All and Unfold All report that too, and Unfold All threw the view to the top of the document in the first attempt. The reading position is restored right after, as before.
- The same file read again keeps its position, so the view only notes a wholesale replacement that happens away from the top (`contentReplacedBeforeLayout`) and, before the next layout pass, asks the layout manager where the document ends and steps inside if it is past it — the probe the reading anchor uses. No frame is drawn between the probe and the restore.
- A whole-document edit made in one editor of a split is a wholesale replacement for the other one as well. Its place is remembered by line before the edit — paragraph number and column, `captureReadingPlace` — and restored after it with the reading anchor (`settleReadingPlace`), which also runs the past-the-end probe; an editor that was at the end is pinned to the end. For undo and redo every editor of the split remembers its own place, and the one that follows the caret is left alone. The place is restored once more on the next turn, because the syntax-colour revalidation can invalidate the whole document again by then. Sitting at the end, the other editor could also lay out the whole document (23,878 fragments, and an undo of 7.2 s instead of 5.6 s); leaving its probe unarmed made it do just that (2,254 ms to settle).
- Real-use scenarios `layout-switch-shorter`, `layout-switch-shorter-split`, `layout-split-sibling-tool` and `layout-reload-shorter` (`RealUse+Layout.swift`). `layout-split-sibling-tool` runs comment, undo, redo and uncomment with the other editor in the middle and at the end.

### Compatibility

- macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later. The DMG (`HyperEdit-<version>-universal.dmg`) contains one universal binary that runs natively on Apple Silicon (arm64) and Intel (x86_64) Macs.

## [0.11.3](https://github.com/Hyeongcheol-An/HyperEdit/compare/v0.11.2...v0.11.3) (2026-11-26)

### 🐛 Bug Fixes

* **editor:** leave folded lines out of the layout so folded documents scroll right ([#29](https://github.com/Hyeongcheol-An/HyperEdit/issues/29)) ([088a09f](https://github.com/Hyeongcheol-An/HyperEdit/commit/088a09f29eb6b86195ebfea35404df1afe9eab07))


## Release notes

Folded documents scroll and re-flow correctly, Fold All and Unfold All keep the line with the caret where it was, the two editors of a split now share their folds, and keys next to a fold no longer touch lines you cannot see.

### Fixed

- **Scrolling a folded document** — with many folds in a large file, wheel steps jumped by hundreds of points, End left an empty band and lines without numbers, and the document was treated as if nothing were folded. In a fully folded 1.3 MB note a wheel step took 28 ms on average with pauses of up to 1.2 s; it now takes about 8 ms without pauses.
- **Changing the layout with folds** — changing the text size, the sidebar or the window width with everything folded blocked the app for up to a second and could move the view to a different place. The line you were reading stays put and none of these changes stalls.
- **Unfold All** — Unfold All kept the top line of the window in place, so every fold between that line and the caret opened above the caret and pushed its line off the bottom of the screen (from 196 pt to 772 pt down in a 382 pt editor). The caret's line now stays at the same height. When the caret is off screen, the top line is kept as before; unfolding a single region keeps the top line as before.
- **Fold All** — Fold All kept the top line of the window, so the header that the caret moves to was pulled up by every region that folded above it. The caret's line — or, when that line is folded away, the header that takes its place — now stays at the same height (127.3 pt before and after). When the caret is not on screen, the view stays on the place you were reading.
- **Carets and column blocks on hidden lines** — a column block dragged or extended across a fold has a row on every hidden line, and Select All Matches or ⌘D can put carets inside a fold; the fold stayed closed and the next key edited lines that were not on screen. Such a fold now opens. Folding a region that has several carets inside left the hidden ones where they were — and when the main caret was among them, Fold did nothing. Every caret now moves to the end of the header line; a column block collapses to one caret.
- **Files with CR LF line ends** — folding a region with the caret inside it put the caret between the CR and the LF of the header line, so the next character typed split that line end: the document gained a lone CR and a lone LF, and the line numbers below were off by one. Several carets, ↑ across a fold onto its header and ↓ on the header of a fold that reaches the end of the document ended in the same place. The caret now stops before the CR.
- **Writing right below a folded section** — after typing on the line below a folded section and selecting part of it, the fold grew over that line and the next key replaced text that was not on screen. A line that holds a caret, a selection or a block row is no longer folded away. When the section has to open because the line you are writing now belongs to it, that line stays where it is instead of sliding down by the height of the section (below a 60-line section it left the screen).
- **Arrow keys on a wrapped folded header** — Down on the first screen line of a folded header that wraps jumped straight below the fold, so the rest of the header could not be reached with the arrow keys, and Up from a wrapped line below the fold skipped that line's upper screen lines. The caret now steps through the screen lines.
- **Undo and fold arrows** — after undo or redo in a document with nothing folded, the gutter arrows disappeared for a moment and Fold could fold lines that were not a region (lines 10–12 for a caret on line 10 after undoing two lines inserted above). Undoing a line inserted right above a region no longer removes that region's arrow.
- **Scrolling with thousands of folds** — the marker strip beside the scroll bar was redrawn on every scroll step. In a document with 15,000 folds a wheel step took 13.7–16.3 ms; it now takes 5.1 ms.
- **Very large folds** — folding 12,000 lines under one header takes 22 ms, and typing on that header 2.5 ms a key.

### Changed

- **The two editors of a split share their folds.** Folds now belong to the document instead of to one editor: folding or unfolding in one editor of a split does the same in the other. Until now each editor had its own folds. The other editor keeps the place it was showing; if its caret ends up inside a fold, the caret moves to the end of the fold's header line.
- **A key that reaches hidden text opens the fold first.** Moving or extending the selection by a character, a word (⌥→, ⇧⌥→), to the end of a line or paragraph, or by ⇧↑ / ⇧↓ opens a fold as soon as the step would reach its hidden lines, and the key then does exactly what it does in the unfolded document: ⇧⌥→ at the end of a folded header selects the first word of the first hidden line, with the fold now open. Deleting by word or line next to a fold likewise removes just what it removes in the unfolded document, and the fold opens so that you see what went. A step that only reaches the header's own line break (⇧→ at its end) leaves the fold closed, and so does a line-end key that does not move the caret (⌘→ at the end of the header). A key that shrinks a selection is judged by the end it moves, so the fold next to the other end stays closed (⇧← three times from the end of a folded header, then ⇧⌥→). ↑ and ↓ without a selection still skip over a fold. This holds for folds inside folds — every fold the step passes through opens — and for right-to-left paragraphs, where ⌥← moves forward in the text.

### Known limitations

- A fully folded screen still scrolls at two to three times the cost of an unfolded one (5.1 ms against 1.7 ms a wheel step in a document with 15,000 folded two-line regions).
- In a paragraph that mixes left-to-right and right-to-left text, ← and → are checked in both directions, so such a key can open a fold it does not reach.
- For a selection that was not made with the caret keys — a drag, for instance — the first selecting key is checked from both ends, because the editor cannot tell which end will move: the fold next to the end that stays can open.
- Lines that end with a lone CR (the classic Mac format) are not handled: folding breaks lines at LF, with or without a CR before it.
- As before, folds are kept only while the app runs, and a mouse drag across a folded header selects the hidden text.

### For contributors

- Hidden paragraphs used to be laid out as zero-height fragments. TextKit 2 honours that only for fragments it has laid out: every other range is estimated from the paragraph content, and at most 2,000 fragments stay laid out, so a 51,000 pt folded document was measured as 537,651 pt in a windowless test. Hidden paragraphs are now left out of the content storage's element enumeration (`NSTextContentStorageDelegate`'s `shouldEnumerate`), and `FoldingContentStorage` restarts the enumeration past a fold so that their elements are never created (18.3 → 9.2 ms per wheel step in the folded note). The last paragraph of the document stays enumerated, because `NSTextView` cannot size the document without it; it is hidden with a zero-height fragment and a 0.01 pt display paragraph. Fold changes are announced with `edited(.editedAttributes)` — `invalidateLayout(for:)` leaves stale height estimates above the viewport.
- Unfold All in `FoldController` wraps the change in `HyperTextView.preservingReadingPosition(preferCaret: true)`: the reading anchor is the caret line when that line is on screen, and the top line otherwise. Folding and single unfolds keep `preferCaret: false`. `mouse-foldall` measures the caret line's screen position before and after the command.
- `FoldStore` (one per content storage) holds the folds and the candidates; `FoldController` (one per editor) keeps the pills, the gutter, the commands and the reading position. When a command changes the folds, the editor that issued it anchors on its caret line for Fold All and Unfold All; when an edit or a rescan opens a fold, the editor that has the keyboard focus does.
- AppKit moves the caret through the laid-out paragraphs, so its steps jump over a closed fold. Before a caret-step command runs, `openFoldsInReach` works out from the string what the step would reach in the unfolded document and opens the folds that hide it (⇧⌥→ after the `z` of a folded `def third(z):` used to select across the closed fold, and Delete then removed the two hidden lines: 225 → 184 characters, 213 in the open document). `stepIntoFold`, the fix-up after the step, remains for the boundary cases. A step that extends the selection notes every hidden paragraph in its reach, so nested folds all open; a step without a selection opens only what hides the place the caret lands on. The reach starts from the end the previous caret step moved (`caretStepHead`; from both ends when that is not known), and a ← or → command is read in the writing direction of its paragraph (`writingDirections`: the paragraph style, otherwise the first strong character; both ways when the paragraph mixes directions).
- The end of a folded header is the end of the line's text (`FoldStore.headerEnd(before:)`), not the offset before the hidden lines: with CR LF line ends that offset lies between the CR and the LF. The caret moved out of a fold, the anchor of Fold All, the pill and the arrow keys that cross a fold use it, and a one-character step counts CR LF as one step. For lines that end with LF alone every value is the same as before. `fold-crlf` counts the CR LF pairs, lone CRs and lone LFs of the document after each step (53 issues before).
- Undo and redo do not go through `shouldChangeText`; their edits are forwarded to the fold store whenever there is something to move — folds, regions, or a range that still has to be laid out again (`tracksUndoEdits`). A range freed by an edit is kept in post-edit coordinates and moved by the later edits of the same batch; with pre-edit offsets a Replace All above and inside a fold that reached the end of the document left the last line of the file off screen.
- The multi-caret ranges are trusted in the selection watcher only when they match the native selection: in the middle of a multi-caret edit they are still pre-edit offsets while the folds have already moved.
- Real-use scenarios in `RealUse+Fold.swift` (`fold-keys`, `-edit`, `-find`, `-partial`, `-reflow`, `-giant`, `-split`, `-secret`, `-nowrap`), `RealUse+FoldGuard.swift` (`fold-delete`, `-lineends`, `-replace`, `-multi`, `-rescan`, `-enum`) and `RealUse+FoldGuard2.swift` (`fold-multicaret`, `-wordstep`, `-tall`, `-undo`, `-wrapped`, `-strip`, `-nested`, `-rtl`, `-carets-far`, `-crlf`). The guard scenarios press the same keys in the unfolded and in the folded document and compare the results. The self-test counts paragraphs against layout fragments after unfolding, so a line left out of the layout is caught. `scroll-folded` and `layout-fold` went from 100 issues to none and can go back into the full run.

### Compatibility

- macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later. The DMG (`HyperEdit-<version>-universal.dmg`) contains one universal binary that runs natively on Apple Silicon (arm64) and Intel (x86_64) Macs.
- Folds can no longer differ between the two editors of a split.

## Release notes

Word wrap off no longer freezes large documents, and the editor now scrolls sideways properly.

### Fixed

- **Freezes with word wrap off** — turning word wrap off in a 1.3 MB note blocked the app for 1.7–3.4 s, and with wrap off every change of theme, syntax colors or text size froze it again for 1.7–4.4 s. A 4 MB log opened with wrap off took 15 s, and one wheel step in it 13–17 s. None of these stalls is left: the log opens in 0.35 s and scrolls at 3.7 ms a step. Typing with wrap off now costs the same as with wrap on (on a file of long paragraphs it was 17.0 ms a key against 13.9 with wrap on; now 14.4 against 14.5).
- **Sideways scrolling** — with word wrap off the editor often did not scroll sideways at all. In a 3,000-line file whose longest line was 17,334 pt wide the text stayed as wide as the window: no horizontal scroll bar, ⌘→ did not follow the caret to the end of the line, and on a long line far down it threw the view to the top of the document. The scroll range now reaches the widest line shown so far and grows when a longer line comes on screen.
- **Following the caret sideways** — line ends (⌘← / ⌘→), typing and Hangul composition past the right edge, and find results far to the right now bring the caret into view. Moving left one character at a time no longer leaves the caret hidden for one keypress at the left edge.
- **Column blocks** — dragging a column block across a line shorter than the block threw a sideways-scrolled view back to that line's end (from x 600 to x 169), so the block grew to the wrong columns (52–90 instead of 90–102).
- **Text size and reloads** — changing the text size while looking at the end of a long line keeps the same characters in view, in both editors of a split, and a file that another program keeps appending to no longer returns to the first column each time it is read again.

### Known limitations

- The horizontal scroll range covers the widest line shown so far, not the widest line of the document: a longer line further down widens it when it scrolls into view. It does not shrink until you switch to another file or toggle word wrap.
- Coming back to a tab starts at the first column; the sideways position is not kept per file.

### For contributors

- `Editor/HyperTextView+NoWrap.swift`. When the text container does not track the view's width, every read of `-[NSTextView textContainerOrigin]` lays out the whole document — 22–26 ms a call in a laid-out 1.3 MB file, 2.1–2.4 s after anything that invalidates all layout — and viewport layout, frame changes, the insertion point and our own caret geometry read it on every step. Its value is always the container inset, so the getter returns the inset directly while wrap is off (checked against AppKit's value in left-to-right and right-to-left documents and under an Arabic interface: no mismatch).
- AppKit's horizontal auto-sizing is unreliable under TextKit 2, so the view keeps its own width: the wider of the editor and the widest line laid out so far, measured on every viewport layout and before a range is scrolled into view. The text container stays infinitely wide — a finite width sends right-to-left paragraphs to its far edge. A font change rescales the measured width of every editor of the session once, from the primary editor; doing it per editor went wrong in a split in either order.
- Real-use scenarios `nowrap-hscroll`, `-caret`, `-draw`, `-rtl`, `-split`, `-layout`, `-typing` and `-log` (`RealUse+NoWrap.swift`). On the previous code the first set reports 43 functional issues and 1,268 stall rows; now none. Files opened from Finder, the Dock or the command line now open as tabs in the HyperEdit window.

### Added

- **Open With, the Dock and `open -a`** — HyperEdit was already listed under **Open With** for plain-text and Markdown files, but nothing received the file. A file opened from Finder, dropped on the Dock icon or passed with `open -a HyperEdit notes.md` now opens as a tab in the one HyperEdit window. Several files at once get a tab each and the last one is shown; a file that is already open just switches to its tab, and the file you are viewing is not read again. Tabs from the last session stay where they are. A file reached under another spelling of its name (letter case, composed or decomposed Hangul) uses its one tab.

While a macro is playing or a sheet or dialog is open, they open when it is finished. While you are writing a note in the Quick Append panel, they open after the note is appended or the panel is closed, so ⌘↩ cannot land in the wrong file; an empty Quick Append panel does not hold a file back.

### Fixed

- Until now, opening a file from Finder or the Dock while HyperEdit was running did not open the file and added a second window showing the same document. There is one window again.

### Known limitations

- HyperEdit does not bring itself to the front: Finder, the Dock and `open` do that, and `open -g` keeps it in the background on purpose. A minimized window is brought back.
- A file that is not text is refused when it is handed over from outside; it is not passed on to its default app, as it is when you pick it with ⌘O or in the sidebar.

### For contributors

- `ExternalOpen` collects requests from `application(_:open:)` (Finder, the Dock, `open -a`) and hands them to `AppModel.openExternal` once the document may change. Options that take a value are listed in `LaunchOptions.valueOptions` (`optionalValues` for `--split`); `LaunchOptions.value(_:)` answers only for those, so a new option that is missing from the list does not work at all instead of having its value opened as a file.
- **Automated runs are isolated in one place**, `Apps/mac/AutomationRun.swift`. A test build shares its bundle identifier with the installed app, so everything macOS keeps per bundle identifier, and everything it routes by it, is shared too.
  - `isIsolated` (a scripted run or `--ephemeral`) keeps away from the user's preferences, default folder, snapshots, bookmarks, snippets, macros, grammars, saved window state and — new — saved window frames. SwiftUI saves the frame of its window by name whenever the window becomes key, which neither `restorationBehavior(.disabled)` nor `ApplePersistenceIgnoreState` stops; a background self-test changed where the installed app's window opens. Isolated runs replace the `NSWindow` methods that save or read a frame by name before any window exists.

Anything else is refused with `[automation] not opened — outside this run's folders: <path>` in the Output panel and on stderr — a file you open in Finder while a test is running can reach the test instance, and its next edit would be autosaved into your file. Name the fixture folders of new tests `hyperedit-…`.
  - A `--snapshot` run without `--snapshot-activate` therefore captures the window, and a `--quick-append` panel, in their inactive look.
  - Checks: the self-test's `isolation:` lines and the harness scenario `automation-isolated` share one probe (`IsolationProbe`).
- Tests do not send Apple events to the app itself: an open-documents or reopen event to one's own process can activate the app (two times out of three), and `NSApp.deactivate()` did not take it back. `open-external` calls the delegate directly. `open-launch` is launched once more by `scripts/realuse.sh`, with two text files, a folder and a missing path as bare arguments right after `--quit`, and reports into `<out>/open-launch/` (`scripts/realuse.sh --only open-launch`; add `-- --locked` for the launch lock). The script now stops the app it launched when it is interrupted, and lists the files a run refused after the report.

- macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later. The DMG (`HyperEdit-<version>-universal.dmg`) contains one universal binary that runs natively on Apple Silicon (arm64) and Intel (x86_64) Macs.
- No settings or files need migrating.

File tabs: every open file now has its own tab above the editor, modelled on UltraEdit's file tabs.

### New

## Release notes

Secret blocks, HTML / PDF export, multi-line syntax colors with user-defined grammars, caret styles and window opacity, and an app lock.

### New

- **Secret blocks** — a fenced code block whose info string starts with `secret` (```` ```secret bank ````) is masked in the editor as a blank bar with `••••••••`; the fence lines stay visible. Put the caret inside to reveal and edit the block, move out to mask it again. **Reveal Secrets (⌥⌘S)** shows everything and re-masks after 10 / 30 / 60 s (default 30 s) or never. Find results, Find in Files, bookmark previews, the Markdown preview and exports show secret lines as `••••••••`; the Outline and Timeline skip them. Copying text that overlaps a secret line clears the clipboard after 30 s (configurable), and **Edit › Clear Clipboard** clears it at once. This is masking, not encryption: the file and its snapshots stay plain text.
- **Export as HTML / PDF** (File menu) — Markdown is rendered like the preview, notes and code become a syntax-colored block; the HTML is self-contained with no scripts, and the PDF is paginated (Letter or A4 by region, 36 pt margins). The save panel offers the theme and, for notes and code, line numbers.
- **Syntax colors across lines** — block comments, multi-line strings (Swift / Python `"""`, JavaScript template literals, Go raw strings, Rust `r#"…"#`) and Markdown fenced code blocks keep their color until they close, and fences are colored with the language named in the info string. A per-line state table computes a 34k-line file in about 10 ms and updates only the edited lines, so typing stays at about 2–3 ms per keystroke.
- **User-defined grammars** — `~/Library/Application Support/HyperEdit/grammars/*.json` adds a language or overrides a built-in one with its name, extensions or file names, comment markers, string delimiters and keyword lists. HyperEdit creates the folder on first launch with an `example.json` and a schema README; **File › Open Grammars Folder…** opens it and **File › Reload Grammars** applies changes without restarting. Toggle Comment (⌘/) follows the grammar.
- **App lock (Touch ID)** — **HyperEdit › Lock HyperEdit (⌃⌘L)** covers the whole window with an opaque lock screen and hides even the file name; **Unlock** authenticates with Touch ID or your login password. Settings › App Lock can require authentication at launch, lock after 1–30 minutes without input, and lock when the Mac sleeps or the screen locks. While locked, typing and menus are blocked, open sheets are closed, revealed secrets are masked again and the Quick Append shortcut asks you to unlock first. It hides the screen (also from VoiceOver); it does not encrypt files.
- **Cursor style and window opacity** (Settings › Appearance) — Bar, Block or Underline caret with blinking on or off, applied to multi-caret and column-mode carets as well; window opacity 50–100 % with an optional blur behind the window, also from View › Window Opacity.

### For contributors

- New developer flags: `--cursor <bar|block|underline>`, `--no-cursor-blink`, `--opacity <0.5–1.0>`, `--blur`, `--reveal-secrets`, `--locked`, `--settings-height <pt>`. The DMG is now named `HyperEdit-<version>-universal.dmg` and release notes are written in English.
- Under TextKit 2, `NSTextView.drawInsertionPoint` is never called (the system insertion indicator draws the caret); the secondary carets of multi-caret and column mode are now drawn from `drawBackground`, which also fixes them not appearing in v0.7.

### Known limitations

- Secret blocks hide text on screen only; the file, autosaves and snapshots are plain text, the mask bar shows each line's length, and a bookmark made while masking was off keeps its plain preview.
- Export writes the whole document; there is no selection or multi-file export, no page-size or font options and no PDF table of contents. PDF export is limited to 2 MB.
- Apart from Rust raw strings, multi-line strings must use the same opening and closing delimiter; one block-comment pair per language; user grammars are word lists without regular-expression rules.
- App lock is a screen lock, not encryption; the idle timer only sees input made in HyperEdit.
- Inside a single paragraph longer than 20,000 characters the Block and Underline carets fall back to the bar caret; secondary carets do not blink.
- A secret fence must start at the left margin (not inside a list item or quote); block comments do not nest; exported HTML keeps image URLs as written, so remote images may be fetched.

### Compatibility

- macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later. The DMG (`HyperEdit-<version>-universal.dmg`) contains one universal binary that runs natively on Apple Silicon (arm64) and Intel (x86_64) Macs.
- No settings or files need migrating. New settings default to the previous behavior (bar caret, opaque window, masking on, app lock off).
